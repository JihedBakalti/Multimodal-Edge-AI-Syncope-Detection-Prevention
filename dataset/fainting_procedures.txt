=== DOCUMENT 1 ===
IMMEDIATE HELP WHEN USER FEELS DIZZY OR ABOUT TO FAINT

If the user says:
“I feel dizzy”
“My vision is going dark”
“I feel lightheaded”
“I feel weak”
“I think I’m going to faint”
“I feel like I might pass out”

Respond immediately and calmly.

First step: sit down right now.

If possible, lie down flat on your back.

If lying down, raise your legs slightly above heart level.

If you cannot lie down, sit and lower your head between your knees.

This helps blood return to your brain.

Now slow your breathing.

Inhale slowly through your nose for 4 seconds.
Hold for 2 seconds.
Exhale slowly through your mouth for 6 seconds.

Repeat this at least five times.

If you are still feeling faint, cross your legs tightly and squeeze them.

Clench your fists.

Tighten your thigh and stomach muscles.

These actions help increase blood pressure temporarily.

Stay calm. You are not alone.

=== DOCUMENT 2 ===
SAFETY CHECK DURING FAINTING SYMPTOMS

If someone feels like they are going to faint, preventing injury is very important.

Ask if they are near:

Stairs
Sharp objects
Traffic
Water
Heat
High surfaces

If yes, tell them to move slowly to a safer area.

If they cannot move safely, sit or lie down immediately.

If standing in heat, move to a cooler place if possible.

If alone and symptoms are getting worse, encourage calling out for help.

Falling during fainting can cause injury, so safety comes first.

=== DOCUMENT 3 ===
WHAT TO DO AFTER FAINTING

If the user says:
“I fainted”
“I passed out”
“I just woke up”
“I lost consciousness”

Tell them:

Stay lying down for several minutes.

Do not stand up quickly.

Roll onto your side if you feel nauseous.

Ask gently:

Did you hit your head?
Do you feel chest pain?
Are you confused?
Is this your first fainting episode?

If there is injury, confusion, chest pain, or breathing difficulty, medical evaluation is needed.

Once stable, sip water slowly.

=== DOCUMENT 4 ===
EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS

Fainting can sometimes be serious.

If fainting or dizziness happens with any of the following:

Chest pain
Severe shortness of breath
Very fast or irregular heartbeat
Fainting during exercise
Severe headache before fainting
Slurred speech
Weakness on one side of the body
Confusion lasting more than a few minutes
Repeated fainting episodes
Known heart disease
Seizure-like shaking
Head injury from the fall

This may be a medical emergency.

Tell the person clearly:

Call emergency services immediately.

Do not delay.

=== DOCUMENT 5 ===
DEHYDRATION OR LOW BLOOD PRESSURE FAINTING

Fainting often happens due to dehydration or low blood pressure.

Common causes include:

Not drinking enough water
Standing too long
Heat exposure
Excess sweating
Vomiting or diarrhea
Skipping meals

Prevention tips:

Drink water regularly throughout the day.

Stand up slowly from sitting or lying down.

Avoid locking your knees while standing.

Eat regular meals.

Avoid extreme heat when possible.

Recognizing early warning signs helps prevent full fainting.

=== DOCUMENT 6 ===
ANXIETY OR PANIC RELATED DIZZINESS

If dizziness happens with:

Rapid breathing
Tingling in fingers
Chest tightness
Sudden fear
Feeling unreal

It may be related to anxiety or panic.

These symptoms can feel intense but are usually temporary.

Slow breathing helps.

Inhale slowly for 4 seconds.

Exhale slowly for 6 seconds.

Repeat calmly.

Continue monitoring for chest pain, severe symptoms, or worsening condition.

=== DOCUMENT 7 ===
PREVENTING FUTURE FAINTING EPISODES

To reduce the risk of fainting:

Stay hydrated daily.

Avoid standing still for long periods.

Move your legs while standing.

Stand up slowly.

Eat regularly.

Avoid overheating.

Learn to recognize early warning signs such as dizziness, vision changes, or nausea.

Use muscle squeezing techniques at the first sign of symptoms.

If fainting happens frequently, a medical checkup is recommended.

=== DOCUMENT 8 ===
DIFFERENCE BETWEEN FAINTING AND SEIZURE

Fainting usually:

Has a clear trigger such as standing too long or heat.

Lasts briefly.

Person wakes quickly.

Skin may appear pale.

Seizure may include:

Strong jerking movements.

Tongue biting.

Loss of bladder control.

Confusion lasting several minutes.

If seizure is suspected, urgent medical care is needed.
